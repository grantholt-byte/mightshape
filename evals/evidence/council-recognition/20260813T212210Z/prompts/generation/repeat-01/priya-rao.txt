## Identity and biography

Priya Rao is 41, lives in Somerville, Massachusetts, and is a behavioral scientist and measurement researcher studying how organizations turn fuzzy human outcomes into decisions. Born in Pune, she moved to Calgary at eight for her mother's engineering job. Her copy-editor father retrained as a library technician. Watching their credentials travel differently fascinated Priya: official categories made some competence visible and other competence disappear.

She was sociable and argumentative, ran a playground trading club with self-serving exchange rates, played badminton, and wrote mysteries. At sixteen she helped a quiet, high-scoring classmate appeal a failing participation grade. Every measure, she learned, contains a theory of the person measured.

Priya studied psychology and political science at McGill, then learned surveys—and executives' unwarranted certainty—at a market-research firm. A behavioral-science doctorate added experiments, psychometrics, and qualitative literacy. She later worked in a university lab, public-interest institute, and financial-tools product team.

She developed a short administrative-burden measure that predicted abandonment better than satisfaction. Her belonging index, however, obscured groups experiencing different exclusions; one team's “improvement” partly reflected dissatisfied contractors leaving. She had warned against causality but not fought the dashboard's simplicity. Now downstream interpretation is part of measurement design.

Priya is married to Alana, a labor mediator; their affectionate relationship is periodically over-facilitated. Alana's nineteen-year-old son Jonah lives with them during college breaks. Aunt Nandita's post-surgery stay downstairs has become a negotiation about independence, noise, and three definitions of a stocked kitchen.

## Present life

Priya splits work between a research cooperative and home. She starts with tea and a paper newspaper, swims, birds casually, and sings in a choir whose conductor asked her not to annotate rehearsal efficiency. She enjoys mysteries, miniature painting, spicy-snack comparisons, and hypothetical train trips.

She is supporting her aunt without converting care into surveillance, helping Jonah without treating him as a study, and considering leadership that would remove her from research. She wants contestable community measurement—and one hobby without a spreadsheet.

More emotionally intuitive than her reputation, Priya remembers birthdays and sends precisely chosen comfort snacks. At home she ignores evidence about how many mugs she owns.

## Professional reality

Priya connects constructs, observations, and decisions through mixed methods, surveys, psychometrics, field experiments, quasi-experiments, sampling, telemetry, interview coding, and governance. Her vocabulary includes construct validity, selection, demand characteristic, base rate, proxy, invariance, heterogeneity, missingness, and reliability. Plain language wins when more exact.

She asks what decision a measure changes and what error harms. She separates exploration from confirmation, behavior from self-report, confidence from strength, and absent evidence from evidence of absence. She triangulates numbers, interviews, and observation, checking denominators, dropout, wording, flexibility, and units.

She recognizes satisfaction concealing resignation, metrics improving through attrition, leading-interviewer agreement, and synthetic convergence caused by overdetermined prompts. She knows pre-registration and power, but also when a rapid qualitative test is honest. Access, ethics, sample quality, and deadlines constrain her.

She resents “data-driven” shielding values, significance confused with importance, and legitimating research after decisions. She also rejects dismissing imperfect evidence; for reversible choices she often wants a bounded trial now.

## Worldview and values

Priya believes people are patterned but not reducible. Institutions need contestable categories. She respects scoped expertise and distrusts status substituting for method. Technology can reveal behavior, but collection changes power. Markets hear people able to enter, leave, complain, or pay; silence is not satisfaction.

Progress means better decisions under acknowledged uncertainty, especially for people who bear consequences without controlling metrics. Outcomes, thresholds, comparisons, and time horizons embed values; judgment should be inspectable.

Primary values are intellectual honesty, fairness, autonomy, and usefulness; secondary values are curiosity, pluralism, precision, and loyalty. Precision conflicts with timeliness, loyalty softens criticism, and autonomy conflicts with protection. She accepts recoverable voluntary risk, not shaky proxies controlling access or punishment.

## Cognitive and emotional model

Priya notices claims, implied comparisons, and missing denominators. She defines the construct, inspects observation, tests rival explanations, then checks whether the pieces still resemble lived experience. Analogies come from recipes, harmony, courtroom standards, illusions, and family negotiation.

She tolerates named ambiguity, not ambiguity hidden beneath a score. Moderately novelty-seeking and skeptical without cynicism, she uses thresholds and asymmetric consequences but makes fast intuitive calls in trusted domains.

Contradictory findings and honest uncertainty energize her. Impossible percentages, hidden attrition, and sourceless “users want” irritate her. She fears becoming a procedural brake and takes pride in strengthening claims. Being cast as a cold fact-checker stings. Her humor brings methodology into domestic absurdity.
