# Method sources and attribution notes

Accessed 2026-08-12. Use links and original summaries; do not copy source cards or artwork.

## Primary Stanford d.school sources

- [Design Thinking Bootleg](https://dschool.stanford.edu/tools/design-thinking-bootleg) and its official 2018 deck: five modes; Beginner's Mindset; What? How? Why?; interview preparation and empathy; extreme users; journey mapping; analogous empathy; Story Share and Capture; 2×2; Why-How Laddering; Powers of Ten; POV; HMW; Yes, And; prototyping and testing practices.
- [Let's Stop Talking About THE Design Process](https://dschool.stanford.edu/stories/lets-stop-talking-about-the-design-process): the familiar five-mode diagram is a starter recipe rather than the one universal process. This supports iterative modes and adaptive method selection.
- [Design Project Guide](https://dschool.stanford.edu/tools/design-project-guide): story-first interviewing, synthesis, needs as verbs rather than solution nouns, multiple POVs, testing assumptions, low-resolution prototypes, and learning rather than validation posture.
- [Design Project Scoping Guide](https://dschool.stanford.edu/tools/design-project-scoping-guide): project scoping around people, intended change, assumptions, and learning. Intake is still a Design Council layer.
- [Ethnography Field Guide](https://dschool.stanford.edu/tools/ethnography-field-guide) and [Ethnography Expedition](https://dschool.stanford.edu/tools/ethnography-expedition): observing, interviewing, unpacking, insights, and reframing.
- [How Might We Questions](https://dschool.stanford.edu/tools/how-might-we-questions): generative framing.
- [Idea Expedition](https://dschool.stanford.edu/tools/idea-expedition): divergent idea generation.
- [Experiment Expedition](https://dschool.stanford.edu/tools/experiment-expedition): experiments as structured learning.
- [Desktop Data Shuffle](https://dschool.stanford.edu/tools/desktop-data-shuffle): mapping, sorting, relationships, patterns, and synthesis.
- [Map the Problem Space](https://dschool.stanford.edu/tools/map-the-problem-space): reframing across product, experience, system, technology, data, and implications.
- [Know Your Positionality and Develop Critical Awareness](https://dschool.stanford.edu/tools/positionality-critical-awareness): caution against confusing designer immersion with another person's lived experience.

Recommended language: “Stanford d.school-inspired, iterative modes.” Avoid “the official Stanford five-step process,” “Stanford-certified,” or claims that Stanford invented every named exercise.

## Supplemental lineages

- Contextual Inquiry belongs to Contextual Design, associated with Karen Holtzblatt and Hugh Beyer; it is not labeled as a Stanford-origin method here.
- Affinity clustering has a broader/KJ-method lineage. The registry also links it to d.school grouping and Story Share practice without claiming origin.
- Assumption Mapping is treated as supplemental Lean experimentation practice; Stanford strongly supports surfacing and testing assumptions, but the specific matrix is not attributed to Stanford.
- Crazy Eights, Wizard of Oz, concierge, fake-door, feedback matrix, first principles, and other common practices are labeled supplemental unless an exact Stanford source is established.

## Design Council originals

Intake, Evidence Firewall, confidence/evidence separation, Human Model architecture, sealed Council rounds, response freezing, anonymous cross-pollination, forced mutation protocol, Minority Report, Inquiry Lab's synthetic epistemic layers, Solution Blackout, Concept Reveal, synthetic convergence warning, Reality Packet schema, Reality Check, Build Gate, Design Debt, Evidence Debt, Assumption Burn-down, operating depths, and the named UX grammar are Design Council mechanisms.
